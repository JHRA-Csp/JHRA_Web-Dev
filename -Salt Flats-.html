The Bonneville Salt Flats in northwestern Utah are a massive, 30,000-acre stretch of salt crust was left behind when the Great Lake Bonnville evaporated. The ground is so flat and covered in hexagonal salt shapes that it looks almost like another planet. The salt Flats sometimes are the home of most land speed compitions happen when race car drivers drive cars with a rocket engine inside it, just to reach new world record of 622.407 mph acheived by the Blue Flame in 1970 and held it for 13 years.
